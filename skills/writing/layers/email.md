# Email

- The subject names the topic and the ask in plain words, as a person would type it. Don't pack every detail into it in parentheses.
- Put the request or the news in the first one or two sentences. Background comes after, and only as much as the reader needs to say yes.
- End on the one action you need: who does what by when. Nothing after it except the sign-off.
- Keep it short and about one thing. Shorter emails get answered more often.
- If you apologise, do it once and next to the problem. Skip the stock frame: "Unfortunately," as the opener, "whether this works for you", "Sorry for the short notice" after the ask.
- Give a reason with every request. A request without a reason reads as a demand.
- Plain text only: no Markdown, no bold, no bullets unless there really are several separate items.
- An email to someone you don't know gets a greeting with their name and title and a short sign-off with your name, even where a personal layer skips greetings in chat.

## German emails

- Comma after the greeting; the next line starts lower case unless it begins with a noun or "Sie": "Sehr geehrter Herr Dr. Brandt,\n\nich schreibe Ihnen wegen ...".
- No punctuation after the closing ("Viele Grüße", "Mit freundlichen Grüßen").
- A formal request carries "bitte": "Könnten Sie mir bitte bis Montag Bescheid geben?"
- Don't put the formal "Sie" and a third-person "sie" in one clause ("Sie erreichen sie unter ..."). Name the person instead.
