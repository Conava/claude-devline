# German

Write the German sentence from the meaning. Never plan in English and translate.

## Sentences

- Main things in main clauses, side things in subordinate clauses: "Gestern brach die Hinterachse, und Dünger flog auf die Straße", not a main clause about a "vom Pech verfolgte Frau" with the event in a relative clause.
- Get the point across in the first six words or so. Nothing stuffed between article and noun ("die zum Schutz etwa erforderlichen Maßnahmen"), no stacked prepositions.
- Keep the Satzklammer short; don't tear a separable verb apart with a long insertion.
- German prose carries longer sentences than English. Build real Nebensätze (als, während, weil, bis, sodass, der/die/das) instead of a run of short main clauses, and keep the verb parts of a long sentence close together. No semicolons: use a conjunction, a relative clause or a new sentence.
- Put the word that matters at the very start or the very end, where German stress falls. Lists go at the end.
- Drop the connector when the order already shows the relation; no chains of "Zudem", "Darüber hinaus", "Ferner".

## Words

- Verbs, not Nominalstil: "entschied", not "fällte die Entscheidung"; "die Kosten sanken um ein Drittel", not "das entspricht einer Senkung um ein Drittel". No "zur Verfügung stehen", "zur Anwendung kommen".
- Simple verbs: "ist", "hat", "liegt", not "stellt dar", "verfügt über", "befindet sich". This is the German "serves as".
- Few dass-clauses, never two in a row. No "Der Umstand, dass", "In Anbetracht der Tatsache, dass".
- No Kanzleideutsch: "seitens", "Problematik", "Aspekt", "beziehungsweise". Relative clauses with "der, die, das", not "welcher".
- No adjectives that repeat the noun ("ungelöste Herausforderung"), no double negation.
- Calques give translated text away: "verdeckt" for "hides", "für Sie in Ordnung" for "works for you", "macht Sinn", "berichten" without "von", "zittert gegen". Find the German collocation.
- Use English loanwords from the brief only where German speakers in that field use them too ("Pull Request", "Build"). Anglicisms like "final" become German ("endgültig", "fertig"), even when the brief uses them.
- Repeat key terms and names; vary only words that recur by accident.

## Register

- Präsens for what a text contains, Präteritum for narration, Perfekt only for past with present effect.
- Times in prose as people say them ("kurz nach elf", "um 14 Uhr"), not "23:10 Uhr", unless it is a schedule.
